# Lexicon Realms: World 2, five starting realms

Owner: world design. Written 2026-11-19, rewritten the same day after Scott's direction: "No need to stick to canon,
focus instead of making an excellent game". **Status: the five starting realms are live** (finish pass, 2026-11-26).
Emberpeak, Valewyn, the Rootbound, Saffron Haven and the Nameless Hollow ship as playable start zones (levels 1 to 6),
each with its own music track and signature mechanic. First-teacher names that this file drafted differently are
locked in data as Corwin (Emberpeak), Maren and Tilly (Valewyn), Ottilie (the Rootbound) and Corvin (the Hollow).
Larchhill (20 to 25), Drovers' Reach (25 to 30) and the Barrow Deep Halls are also live. Sections below may still
describe the original 1 to 5 proposal where they have not been updated. `ZONES_QUESTS.md` still covers Willowmere, the
valley, the Downs and the High Moor.

Also decided today and assumed here: zones about 3x bigger (about 2 minutes to walk across), levels 1 to 15, angled
3D, mounts later for gold, groups of 2 to 3 foes from about level 4, up to 4 friends online later, mostly solo.

Lexicon Quest is used as a toolbox: its places, people and ideas appear where they make the game better. Timing,
spoilers and canon do not limit anything here.

---

## 0. What makes a starting realm memorable

WoW's best starting zones share four things. Every realm below is built to have all four:

1. **A first impression in the first 30 seconds.** One view that says "you are somewhere new": a skyline, a color, a
   sound.
2. **A local villain with a face**, named in the first 5 minutes, seen from afar by level 3, fought at level 5.
3. **One signature mechanic or set piece** that no other realm has, so the five feel like five games.
4. **A payoff at level 5**: a boss you were warned about, then a journey out that feels like leaving home.

The five are spread across the map's biomes and palettes on purpose: red volcanic mountain, teal flooded lake, glowing
underground roots, gold desert, grey fog town. Each one suits its class's fantasy, but any class can start in any realm
(section 3).

## 1. The five starting realms (levels 1 to 5)

Each realm: about 2 minutes edge to edge, a safe hub with a lantern, a vendor and the class's first teacher, 7 quests,
3 to 4 foe families of its own, single foes until level 3, pairs from level 4, and a boss at level 5.

### 1.1 Warden: Emberpeak, the Forge Mountain

- **First impression.** You arrive on a switchback road at dusk. Below, a town of forges cut into a black mountain,
  rivers of lava glowing between the streets, chimneys breathing sparks into a red sky. The great Ember Gate, a wall of
  iron across the only pass, is the first thing you are told to protect.
- **Palette and mood.** Black basalt, ember orange, ash grey, brass. Constant warm glow from below, drifting ash,
  the ring of hammers. Loud, proud, under siege.
- **First teacher.** Captain Harl of the Gate Watch (LQ's Harl, recast). **Key NPCs:** Garrick, a young smith not yet
  gone to Willowmere; Bess, who runs Bellows Square's canteen and every rumor in it.
- **Villain.** **Brask the Bellowsman**, a foreman who sealed himself in the Ashen Foundry with the Ashen Crew and woke
  the old furnace golems to take the town for himself. His furnace light is visible from everywhere in the realm.
- **Foe families.** Cinderlings (small fire sprites that burst on death: step back), ash hounds (pairs from level 4),
  slag golems (armored, slow, hit hard), Ashen Crew raiders (mixed melee and fire-throwers).
- **Signature: the lava rivers and the Gate.** Lava flows across three roads crust over on a 40 second cycle; you cross
  while they are black and wait while they glow. At level 5 the set piece **Hold the Gate**: three waves of raiders hit
  the Ember Gate while the Gate Watch fights beside you. Hold the line, then go in after Brask.
- **Quests.**
  1. Sparks on the Road (kill): six cinderlings on the switchbacks; learn to step out of the burst.
  2. Bellows Square (explore): Bess's canteen, Garrick's forge, the Gate, the lava crossing.
  3. Hound Season (kill): eight ash hounds above the town; the first pairs.
  4. Cool Iron (collect): six quench stones from the far side of a lava crossing for Garrick.
  5. The Ashen Crew (kill): raiders at the Foundry's outer works; a fire-thrower teaches interrupts.
  6. Hold the Gate (escort, defense): three waves at the Ember Gate with the Gate Watch.
  7. The Bellowsman (kill, boss): Brask and his furnace golem in the Foundry yard. Payoff: the furnaces go dark, the
     town cheers, and Harl sends you down the mountain by ore cart to Willowmere.
- **Why this is fun.** It is the tank's fantasy in its purest form: stand in the gate, hold the line, and the whole
  town is watching.

### 1.2 Mender: Valewyn, the Drowned Shore

- **First impression.** A lake town half underwater. Rooftops and bell towers stick out of shallow teal water; rope
  bridges join them; a sunken keep rises from the middle of the lake. As you watch, the tide pulls back and a street
  appears.
- **Palette and mood.** Teal, silver, sea-glass green, white sails, wet stone. Gulls, creaking ropes, the hush of the
  tide. Beautiful and in trouble.
- **First teacher.** Sister Orla of the Tide Chapel (LQ's Orla, recast). **Key NPCs:** Nella, a ferrywoman who names
  her boats by the sound of their ropes; Lark, her daughter, always where she should not be.
- **Villain.** **The Brine Widow**, a drowned noblewoman whose grief keeps raising the water. She calls the drowned up
  out of the streets at every high tide. Her keep is the island in the middle of the lake.
- **Foe families.** Mudsnappers (crabs that shell up), bog leeches (latch on and drain until pulled off), the drowned
  (slow, they rise at high tide), brine eels in the channels (hit from the water).
- **Signature: the tide.** Every 3 minutes the lake rises and falls. Low tide opens streets, shortcuts and treasure;
  high tide floods them, brings the drowned and strands people on rooftops. **Rescue** objectives: heal or bandage
  stranded villagers before the water reaches them (Menders heal them; every other class uses tide-bandages from the
  chapel).
- **Quests.**
  1. Wet Feet (explore): the rope bridges, the chapel, the low-tide market.
  2. Snappers in the Nets (kill): eight mudsnappers along the fishing nets.
  3. Leech Season (collect): six leech-clean rope coils; leeches latch on, pull them off.
  4. Stranded (rescue): five villagers on rooftops as the tide comes in.
  5. Lark on the Keep Steps (escort): bring Lark back across the causeway before the tide turns, drowned in pairs.
  6. Tide Bells (explore): ring three drowned bells at low tide to weaken the Widow's hold.
  7. The Brine Widow (kill, boss): on the keep steps as the water drains; she floods half the arena on a timer. Payoff:
     the lake falls a hand's width for good, a street comes back for the first time in years, and Nella ferries you
     upriver to Willowmere.
- **Why this is fun.** The world breathes: the same street is a road, then a lake, then a road again, and the healer is
  the one who gets everyone off the rooftops.

### 1.3 Scribe: the Rootbound, under the Great Willow

- **First impression.** You walk down a stair into the roots of a willow so big that a whole library grew inside it.
  A cavern of roots and shelves stretches out of sight, lit by rivers of glowing blue ink and floating pages that drift
  like snow. Words written on the walls light up as you pass.
- **Palette and mood.** Deep green, parchment gold, glowing ink blue, warm lamp amber. Quiet, echoing, wonderful, with
  something chewing far below.
- **First teacher.** Iris, Head Archivist. **Key NPCs:** Mira, her apprentice, who counts everything; Quill, who copies
  every name twice; Hollis, the old Keeper who stayed down (LQ's Deep Stacks).
- **Villain.** **The Archivore**, a starved keeper-beast grown huge on eaten names, living in the willow's heart. It
  sends its bookmites to strip the shelves. You hear it before you see it.
- **Foe families.** Bookmites (swarms of small foes), ink slimes, animated tomes (casters that read spells at you),
  shelf golems (moving shelves that guard aisles).
- **Signature: hidden ink.** Every player carries a Keeper's lantern from quest 1. Its light reveals hidden ink: bridges
  across root chasms, doors in walls, words that point to treasure. The shelves themselves move on a slow cycle,
  opening and closing aisles. The realm is a playground of secrets.
- **Quests.**
  1. Speak, and Be Kept (explore): say your name at the door; the lantern; the Catalog Halls.
  2. Mites in the Margins (kill): eight bookmites; learn to fight a swarm.
  3. Loose Pages (collect): six pages from ink slimes; Quill copies them twice.
  4. Bridges of Ink (explore): cross three hidden ink bridges to reach the Reading Galleries.
  5. Read the Room (kill): animated tomes in the Galleries; interrupt their reading.
  6. The Echo Warden (kill, elite-lite): a mirrored knight that guards the stair down, repeating its last order.
  7. The Eater of Names (kill, boss): the Archivore in the willow's heart, pages falling like snow. Payoff: every word on
     the walls lights up at once, Mira finds a written list in its nest, and Iris sends you up the root stair into
     Willowmere's square, right next door.
- **Why this is fun.** Exploration is the reward: the lantern turns every wall into a maybe, and the caster gets a
  realm where reading is power.

### 1.4 Wayfinder: Saffron Haven, the Sand Sea

- **First impression.** You step off a caravan at an oasis town of saffron domes and white sails. Beyond it, dunes to
  the horizon, a sand-ship gliding over them under full canvas, and far off a wall of storm.
- **Palette and mood.** Saffron, ochre, turquoise water, white linen, violet dusk. Heat shimmer, wind chimes, a big
  sky. Open, bright, adventurous.
- **First teacher.** Sava the Trailmaster (LQ's Sava, recast). **Key NPCs:** Tess, a courier who crosses the desert
  with letters and refuses to lose one; Nico, a sand-ship deckhand.
- **Villain.** **Ravi Scorchwind**, captain of the Scorch Riders, raiders who strike from inside the sandstorms. She
  rides a dune lion everyone in town is afraid of.
- **Foe families.** Sand scorpions (poison over time), dune hyenas (pairs from level 4; they flee and come back), dust
  devils (spinning elementals that knock you back), Scorch Riders (raiders on foot, archers).
- **Signature: storms and the hunt.** Sandstorms roll across the map on a timer, shrinking sight to a few meters; the
  chime posts let you navigate by sound and raiders strike from the storm. The **hunt**: follow fresh tracks across the
  dunes to a beast's lair (tracks glow for the hunter's eye; every class can see them, the Wayfinder sees them further).
  Travel set piece: ride the sand-ship across the sand sea.
- **Quests.**
  1. Off the Caravan (explore): the oasis, the sail docks, the chime post road.
  2. Stingers (kill): eight sand scorpions round the oasis wells.
  3. The Lost Satchel (collect): Tess's six letters, scattered by a dust devil.
  4. Laughing in the Dunes (kill): eight dune hyenas; they run and come back, so be ready.
  5. Follow the Tracks (explore, hunt): track the dune lion's prints to its lair and mark it.
  6. Through the Storm (escort): walk a water caravan through a sandstorm, raiders striking from the storm.
  7. Scorchwind (kill, boss): ride the sand-ship to the raider camp; Ravi and her dune lion. Payoff: the storm wall
     breaks, the town's sails go up, and the sand-ship carries you to the river port and on to Willowmere.
- **Why this is fun.** Wide open space, a beast to hunt, a storm to read, and a ship to ride: the ranger's fantasy of
  being the one who knows the way.

### 1.5 Inkblade: the Nameless Hollow

- **First impression.** A town in fog at night. Every sign is blank. Lamplit guards with lanterns walk slow rounds; the
  rooftops are empty and close together. Someone on a balcony whispers: "Stay off the streets."
- **Palette and mood.** Grey, violet, slate blue, single warm lamp ambers. Night, fog, rain on tiles, footsteps. Tense,
  sly, a little funny.
- **First teacher.** Sloe, who runs the rooftops. **Key NPCs:** Mayor Elian, who cannot remember the town's name and
  hates it; Vessa, a fence who trades in things people forgot they owned.
- **Villain.** **The Faceless Steward** of the Court of Mirrors, who stole the town's name and keeps it locked in a
  mirror. His grey watchmen enforce a curfew.
- **Foe families.** Grey watchmen (patrol with lantern sight cones, call an alarm), hollow ravens (perched spotters
  that wake the watch), mirror shades (copies of you that hit back), the faceless (forgotten townsfolk who turn
  hostile in the fog).
- **Signature: the stealth district.** Lantern cones are visible on the ground; stay out of them and you can walk past
  anything. Rooftop routes, drainpipes and laundry lines make a second map above the streets. Get seen and the watch
  converges. Every class can sneak (the Inkblade's Fade just makes it easy). Fog banks drift through: inside one, foes
  forget you and drop their chase.
- **Quests.**
  1. Stay off the Streets (explore): the first rooftop route to Sloe's attic.
  2. Ravens (kill): eight hollow ravens on the chimneys; drop a spotter before it squawks.
  3. Things People Forgot (collect): six lost keepsakes from the faceless for Vessa.
  4. Curfew (explore): cross the town square past three watch patrols without being seen.
  5. Lift the Keys (collect): steal the Court's mirror keys from watch captains, in pairs.
  6. Names for the Mayor (escort): get Mayor Elian across the rooftops to the bell tower.
  7. The Faceless Steward (kill, boss): in the Court of Mirrors he splits into reflections; strike the one that casts a
     shadow. Payoff: you break the mirror, the town's name rings out from the tower, every sign fills in, and the fog
     lifts onto the Fading Road west to Willowmere.
- **Why this is fun.** A heist town: two maps in one, guards you can read, and the pleasure of walking past danger.

The five realms sit on five sides of Willowmere and each one's level 5 payoff ends in a journey that lands you there:
the ore cart down from Emberpeak, Nella's ferry up from Valewyn, the root stair up from the Rootbound, the sand-ship and
river port from Saffron Haven, the Fading Road out of the fog from the Hollow. Each journey is a short ride (about a
minute, no fighting, the camera free), so it reads as travel without asking you to walk a desert at level 5. Once you
have made the trip, the route back home stays open by the same ride from the capital.

### 2.2 The capital: Willowmere and the Vale (levels 5 to 8)

Willowmere grows from a village into a **capital**: a river city of willows, painted lantern-signs and canals, the
twelve-bell tower in Lantern Square, and the Great Willow rising over the north wall with the Rootbound under it. All
five orders keep a hall here (the town masters, section 2.5), plus the bank, the bells, the trainers, the stables
(mounts, later) and the inn where Hob writes your name twice.

**The convergence moment: the Lantern Council (level 5 to 6).** Every realm's boss dropped one thing: a page written
on cheap paper in a cold hand, listing names. Brask had one, the Widow had one, the Archivore's nest held one, Ravi had
one in her saddlebag, the Steward kept one behind his mirror. Your breadcrumb sends you to Elder Mael at the well. He
lays your page beside four others that other newcomers brought (in a group of friends, your own pages) and they fit
edge to edge: five pages of one list, and on the next page, more names. Someone is writing lists, and someone sent them
to five corners of the world. That is the thread to level 15.

**Willowmere Vale** (the countryside around the city, today's valley grown 3x): meadows, the river and stone bridge,
the pilgrim road, the Whisperwood edge. 7 quests: briar wolf pairs on the pilgrim road, Lark lost in the Whisperwood
(escort), Cobb's hens, Pell's crate to Penny, Garrick's thornback hides, the first groups of three at the wood's edge,
and the breadcrumb west to Thornfold.

- **Why this is fun.** It is the "you made it" moment: the first big city, the first time you see the other four
  callings, the first gear upgrade at a real smith, and the realization that your villain was only one page.

### 2.3 Shepherd's Downs (levels 8 to 11)

Rolling grass under a high sky, drystone walls, sheep, and on the north skyline the Watchtower you will reach at 14.
Thornfold is the hub (Brindle at the fold gate, Tamsin's loom house). Today's Downs quests move here mostly as they are
(q11 to q17, re-leveled), plus:
- **Night at the Stones.** A ring of twelve standing stones with a thirteenth blank one apart. At dusk barrow shades
  rise in the ring and the zone changes: more foes, better drops, a different music. Day and night become a reason to
  come back.
- **The Wethers' Barrow**, a 3-room mini-dungeon under the stones: soloable with care, easier with friends, ending in
  the Barrow Reeve, who counts names and always comes up one short. One of the list's names is carved on his door.
- **Why this is fun.** Freedom: a huge open zone after five tight realms, with packs of three, a zone that changes at
  night, and the first small dungeon.

### 2.4 The High Moor (levels 11 to 15)

A wide heath under a bigger sky: gorse, heather, grey rock, a black tarn, nine chime posts across it, and the broken
Watchtower always on the skyline. Today's Moor quests (q18 to q24) move here, re-leveled, plus:
- **Weather:** mist banks roll across on a timer; mist wolves vanish into them and strike out of them.
- **Telegraphs:** gorse boars paw the ground before they charge; side-step or be stunned.
- **The Watchtower** at the top: armored constructs, watch lamps that heal them, and the capstone elite **the Sentinel
  of the Long Watch** (level 14 to 15). It guards the room where the list-writer was writing: the last page is still
  on the desk, with your own name on it. The game's level 15 ending sends that page back to Mael, and the story to
  whatever comes next.
- **Why this is fun.** A goal you can see from two zones away, weather that changes how you fight, and one careful,
  learnable fight that uses your whole kit.

## 3. Playing with friends

Friends who pick different classes start in different realms. Options:

| option | how it works | for | against |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Choose your start realm | At creation the realm defaults to your class's, but any class may pick any realm. Realm quests work for every class (rescues use bandages, stealth uses everyone's sneak). The level 3 lesson arrives as a letter from your order, opened at any lantern | together from minute one; costs little; solo players get the choice too | you meet your own class's teacher in person only at the capital |
| B. Lantern call | In a party, touch a lantern to travel to a friend's | fixes "we started apart" at any time | skips the travel the realms are built around |
| C. Meet at the capital | Everyone starts at home and meets at level 5 | zero work | the first 40 minutes apart |

**Recommendation: A, with the host's realm as the party default.** When friends create characters inside one room,
everyone starts in the host's realm unless they change it. It keeps the walking, works offline and online the same
way, and four friends of four classes are standing at the same hub in the first minute. Add B later only if playtests
show people miss each other after level 5.

Small rules for any option: kill and collect credit is shared in a party, escorts wait for the slowest member, rescue
targets scale their danger to party size, and the level 5 boss gets a little more health per extra player.

## 4. Pacing, level 1 to 15

Minutes per level follow PROGRESSION 1 (about 185 minutes of pure levelling); the bigger zones add walking, which
mounts (later) will win back.

| level | minutes | where | quests in this stretch | new thing introduced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | your realm | 2 | move, target, two abilities, lanterns, the realm's first view |
| 2 | 7 | your realm | 1 to 2 | loot, the vendor, bags, collect quests |
| 3 | 8 | your realm | 1 | the level 3 lesson; the realm's signature mechanic (lava, tide, ink, storm, stealth) |
| 4 | 10 | your realm | 1 to 2 | foes in pairs; rescue, escort or hunt |
| 5 | 11 | your realm, then the ride | 1 (boss) | first talent point; the realm boss; the journey to the capital |
| 6 | 12 | Willowmere | 2 to 3 | the capital, town masters, the bank; the level 6 ability; the Lantern Council |
| 7 | 13 | Willowmere Vale | 3 | groups of three; group credit with friends |
| 8 | 14 | Vale, then the Downs | 2 to 3 | Thornfold hub; open-zone questing; casters that need interrupts |
| 9 | 15 | Shepherd's Downs | 3 | night at the Stones (day and night) |
| 10 | 16 | Shepherd's Downs | 3 | the level 10 ability; the Barrow mini-dungeon; mounts (later phase) |
| 11 | 17 | Downs, then the Moor | 2 to 3 | weather: mist banks |
| 12 | 18 | the High Moor | 3 | telegraphed charges; foes that blink away |
| 13 | 19 | the High Moor | 3 | armored constructs; foes that heal each other |
| 14 | 20 | the Watchtower | 2 | the level 14 ability; the Sentinel elite |
| 15 | cap | anywhere | 1 | the ending page to Mael; XP becomes gold; a prompt to try another realm |

Quests per zone: each realm 7; Willowmere and the Vale 8 (the Council plus 7); Shepherd's Downs 12; the High Moor 14.
A single character does 41; the game holds 69.

| Foe families | 15 | about 31, plus 5 realm bosses | 16 new (4 per realm, minus the ink slime and wraith reused by the Rootbound) |
| Set pieces | 1 elite | 5 realm bosses, 5 travel rides, Hold the Gate, the Lantern Council, night at the Stones, the Barrow, the Sentinel | |
| New systems | | the lava cycle, the tide, hidden ink and moving shelves, storms and tracks, stealth cones and fog, day and night, weather | each used by one realm or zone, so each is small |

### 5.1 Suggested build order

1. **The Rootbound and the capital.** They touch (the root stair), Willowmere already exists, and the hidden-ink
   mechanic is the cheapest signature. This proves the 3D pipeline, the bigger zone scale, a level 5 boss, a travel
   ride and the Lantern Council with one realm. Ship the realm picker with one realm listed.
2. **The Nameless Hollow.** The stealth district is the riskiest system; build it early to learn whether it is fun.
3. **Emberpeak.** Wave defense and the lava cycle; the tank needs its realm before the shared zones are tuned.
4. **Valewyn.** The tide changes the map itself; build it once the map tools are solid.
5. **Saffron Haven.** Storms and the sand-ship; the most open map, built last of the realms.
6. **Willowmere Vale and the Downs at full size**, then **the High Moor and the Watchtower**, re-leveled.

Each step leaves a playable game: after step 1 every class can play the Rootbound to level 5 and the Vale beyond it
(option A).
